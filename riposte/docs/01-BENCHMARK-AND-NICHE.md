### Top 10 failure patterns

1. **Pay-to-win collections** (Gods Unchained, many gacha CCGs). Riposte sells no power.
2. **Power creep** forcing purchases (Hearthstone eras, Shadowverse). Riposte: expansions add sideways options and new factions, never higher numbers on the curve.
3. **Long matches on mobile** (Duelyst, full-board Master Duel). Fights target under 5 minutes.
4. **Opaque RNG** (pack odds, hidden rolls). Every chance is shown with its odds; enemy traps show their trigger.
5. **New-player walls** (Yu-Gi-Oh! rulings, MTG priority). The chain resolves automatically; only set cards respond, so there are no priority prompts.
6. **Grind-locked meta** (gacha dailies). Unlocks come from playing, any run counts, and the first 2 characters unlock within about 3 runs.
7. **Live PvP with too few players** (Storybook Brawl, Duelyst, LoR PvP). Riposte is single-player first; PvP is async.
8. **Prompt fatigue** (MTG Arena stops). Riposte never asks "respond?"; you pre-commit by setting cards.
9. **Content droughts** (Underlords, Auto Chess). A small team must plan cadence it can hit.
10. **Hostile monetization reviews** (energy timers, ads in premium). No energy, no forced ads.

## Phase 2: Find the niche

### Market map

Axis X: **solo ↔ competitive PvP**. Axis Y: **short session (≤5 min decisions) ↔ deep session (30+ min)**.

```
            DEEP (30+ min)
                 │
  StS, Monster   │  MTG Arena, Master Duel,
  Train, Balatro,│  Netrunner, Gwent
  Inscryption,   │
  Hades          │       ◆ GAP: deep interaction
     ★ RIPOSTE   │         without live PvP
  (chain-stack   │
   roguelike)    │
SOLO ────────────┼──────────────── PvP
                 │
  Card Crawl,    │  Marvel Snap, Clash Royale,
  Archero,       │  Pocket, Duel Links
  Night of Full  │
  Moon           │  Super Auto Pets, The Bazaar (async)
                 │
            SHORT (≤5 min)
```

The empty quadrant is **solo, medium-depth, with real card-on-card interaction**. Roguelike deckbuilders give depth but enemies never *respond* to you: they act on a script. TCGs give interaction but need an opponent, long rules and money.

### Five candidate niches

| # | Pitch | Player | Demand evidence | Why unserved | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Chain-stack roguelike**: a StS-style run where enemies set face-down traps and you answer with your own, resolved as a Yu-Gi-Oh!-style chain | 18–40, played YGO/MTG as a teen, now plays StS/Balatro on phone; frustrated that TCGs need time, money and opponents | YGO is among the best-selling TCGs; Master Duel and Duel Links show digital YGO demand; StS/Balatro show premium roguelike demand on mobile (Balatro 5M+ sold); "roguelike with traps/counters" is a recurring request in deckbuilder communities | Almost no roguelike deckbuilder lets enemies *react*; chains are thought to be too complex for phones | Rules complexity; UI clarity of chains on a small screen |
| 2 | **Run deck becomes a collectible** that you take into async PvP | Collector-competitive | Super Auto Pets, The Bazaar, Backpack Battles prove async ghost PvP | No deckbuilder feeds a persistent collection | Needs backend and balance across power levels |
| 3 | **Co-op roguelike TCG for 2 phones** | Couples, friends | StS2 added 4-player co-op; Across the Obelisk, Hellcard | Few co-op deckbuilders on mobile | Netcode, matchmaking, smaller audience |
| 4 | **Daily-seeded competitive runs** with global boards | Wordle crowd | Spelunky dailies; StS daily climb | Exists inside StS but not as the core | Thin alone; a feature, not a game |
| 5 | **Fully fair collection**, cosmetics only | Burned-out gacha players | Reviews of gacha CCGs; LoR's goodwill | Business pressure pushes to packs | Monetization ceiling |

### Decision

**Build #1, and fold #2, #4 and #5 in as features.** The chain stack is the one mechanic nobody in the roguelike space owns, it is cheap to build (rules, not content), and it works offline. Niches 2, 4 and 5 become features of the same game without needing a separate product:

- Daily Duel (niche 4) is a seed plus a modifier: shipped in the prototype.
- Legacy decks (niche 2) are minted after a boss win and already return as Echo elites offline; async PvP is the same Echo system fed by other players' decks once a backend exists.
- Monetization is premium with cosmetics (niche 5): no packs, ever.
